Code inspection of `generate_calibrated_satellite_surface()` reveals the exact mathematical formula used to produce all four GeoTIFF files:

lons = np.linspace(BBOX[0], BBOX[2], GRID_W) # 77.5 to 81.0 E

lon_grid, lat_grid = np.meshgrid(lons, lats)

forest_factor = np.clip((lat_grid - 18.5) * 0.4 + 0.3, 0.1, 0.8)
river_valley = np.exp(-((lat_grid - 18.8)**2 + (lon_grid - 79.2)**2) / 0.5)

# 1. Pre-monsoon (May) Synthetic Surface Reflectance

# 2. Peak Monsoon (August) Synthetic Surface Reflectance
red_aug = 0.08 + 0.03 * (1.0 - forest_factor) - 0.02 * river_valley
nir_aug = 0.52 + 0.15 * forest_factor + 0.10 * river_valley + 0.05 * np.cos(lat_grid * 3.0)
ndvi_aug = compute_ndvi(nir_aug, red_aug)

# 3. Drought Stress Scenario
red_drought = red_aug + 0.06
nir_drought = nir_aug - 0.12

# 4. Anomaly Map
ndvi_anomaly = ndvi_drought - ndvi_aug

## 6. Known Scientific Limitations

1. **Spatial Covariance Across Districts:** The 6 Telangana pilot districts are geographically contiguous and share synoptic weather systems. In any single year, they typically experience similar regional monsoon anomalies (all 6 were wet in 2020–2022; all 6 were dry in 2015). The effective degrees of freedom in the test set is approximately 6 synoptic seasons, not 36 independent samples.
2. **Missing Indian Ocean Dipole (IOD) Predictor:** The 2023 El Niño false alarm highlights that ENSO alone is insufficient for Indian monsoon prediction. A positive IOD can completely decouple peninsular Indian rainfall from Pacific warming.
3. **Pre-Monsoon Spatial Resolution:** Gridded NASA POWER observations ($0.5^\circ \times 0.5^\circ$) smooth local convective storm cells.
4. **Biophysical Simulation Rasters:** The local GeoTIFF files demonstrate spatial processing workflows but do not represent ground-truthed canopy reflectance measurements.
